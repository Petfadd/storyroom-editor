DOCUMENTARY PRODUCTION SCRIPT — REUSABLE TEMPLATE (Beat-based)

WHAT THIS IS
This is the working format for a documentary whose picture is chosen beat by beat from
your own footage, with narration over it and the real spoken moments left in. It is the
format the storyboard reads directly, so no separate block map or conversion step is
needed. It follows the structure that worked on the finished project "qqqqqqqq", with the faults
that project still carried closed: two of its beats asked for footage past the end of the
file they named, and its picture needed reusing the same shot. Both are now caught before
a build instead of during one.

Read the RULES section once, then fill in the TWO PARTS. Delete every line in square
brackets; they are instructions, not script.

================================================================================
PART 1 — THE NARRATION TABLE (this becomes your SRT)
================================================================================

The storyboard needs an SRT. You can upload an .srt file, or you can let the script
provide one: a table shaped like the one below is read automatically, and its wording,
order and timing are copied, never rewritten. Use exactly these three columns.

Narration blocks:

| Block | Narration timeline | Wording |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 00:00:00,000 → 00:00:04,000 | [the exact words spoken in this block] |
| 2 | 00:00:04,000 → 00:00:09,400 | [the exact words spoken in this block] |
| 3 | 00:00:09,400 → 00:00:15,000 | [the exact words spoken in this block] |
| 4 | 00:00:15,000 → 00:00:21,750 | [the exact words spoken in this block] |

Table rules — each one is checked:

1. Every block's start is the previous block's end. No holes, no overlaps. A few
   milliseconds of rounding is snapped and recorded; anything larger refuses the table.
2. Block numbers run 1, 2, 3 … with none skipped.
3. Block 1 starts at 00:00:00,000.
4. The last block's end equals the duration of your narration audio, to within a quarter
   of a second. This is the most common mistake: the table stops short of the audio and
   the closing words have no block.
5. Wording is what is actually spoken — one block, one stretch of speech. Do not number
   them Srt001/Srt002; plain numbers are what the table reader expects.

If the SRT already exists separately, upload it and leave this table out. If you use both,
the uploaded .srt wins and the table is ignored.

================================================================================
RULES — READ THIS ONCE, THEN KEEP TO IT
================================================================================

THE ONE RULE THAT BREAKS EVERYTHING ELSE
Every Source range must fit inside the file it names. If your script asks for footage
past the end of a video, the storyboard cannot be built, and no amount of review will fix
it. Before you upload, check each beat: Source out must be less than or equal to the
length of that file. The app now reports this on upload — but the fix has to happen in
your script.

THE NARRATION CLOCK IS A SINGLE CONTINUOUS CLOCK
It starts at 00:00:00,000 and it never skips while a bite plays — it is paused, not
advancing. Write it like this:

  - first narration beat:  Narration clock: 00:00:00,000 --> 00:00:33,600
  - the bite:              Narration clock: PAUSED at 00:00:33,600
  - the next narration:    Narration clock: RESUMES at 00:00:33,600 --> 00:02:27,166

So every RESUMES begins with the exact time the PAUSED line named. The final narration
OUT equals your narration file's duration. Missing that is what leaves the closing words
with no beat.

THE MASTER CLOCK IS THE FINISHED DOCUMENTARY CLOCK
It runs from 00:00:00,000 to the total runtime and it does not pause. A narration beat
takes up exactly the narration it covers. A bite takes up its own duration. So each beat
starts where the previous one ended.

Picture length must equal timeline length. For a narration beat:
  (Narration out − Narration in) = (Source out − Source in) = (Master out − Master in)
For a bite:
  (Source out − Source in) = Duration
  (Master out − Master in) = Duration

THE DURATION FIELD ON A BITE
Write it on the Master timeline line as shown, either as `(Duration: 32.5 sec)` or
`(Duration: 1 min 06 sec)`, or on its own line as `Duration: 00:00:32,500`. Whichever you
use, it must equal Source out minus Source in exactly. If it does not, the beat is
refused.

TIMECODES
Always HH:MM:SS,mmm — `00:01:15,600`. Two digits for hours, minutes and seconds, exactly
three for milliseconds. A missing column or a dot instead of a comma will stop the read.

FILENAMES
Use the exact uploaded name, with extension, on one line, with no path and no folder:
`Full source: S01.mp4`. Never `./S01.mp4`, `footage/S01.mp4` or a wildcard. If the name
does not match an uploaded file exactly, the beat is refused, by design.

THE FIVE FIELDS THAT MUST BE EXACTLY RIGHT
  Type                VOICE OVER  or  DOCUMENTARY SOUND BITE
                      (NARRATION (VOICE OVER) is also accepted)
  Narration state     PLAYING / RESUMING on narration; PAUSED on a bite
  Footage audio       MUTED (0%) on narration; UNMUTED (…) on a bite
  Source in / out     inside the real file, with the right span
  Narration clock     continuous, as described above

ONE FIELD, ONE LINE
Do not write any field twice in the same beat — two Source in lines is a hard error, not a
last-one-wins. Blank lines and `--------` separators between beats are fine and ignored.

EVERY BEAT IS A SINGLE BLOCK
Begin with `Beat: 001`, then the fields, then the next `Beat: 002`. Number them in order
with no duplicates and no gaps, and nothing else may start a line with `Beat:` — that word
begins a new beat. Other notes are safe: `Continuity note:` or `Note:` are read as
editorial text and ignored by the checks.

ORDER OF BEATS
Start with narration, end with narration, and put every bite between narration. Two bites
in a row are allowed only when they share the same pause time, which is how a two-voice
exchange is written (Template D).

REUSING A SHOT
A shot may come back later under different narration — that is normal documentary
practice. The app lists every repeated range and asks you to approve it before building.
It is a note, not an error. Do not repeat an *identical* range twice: showing the exact
same frames twice is refused, because it means the picture could not cover the words.

ON-SCREEN CAPTIONS
`On-screen caption:` is the literal text burned into the picture. Captions are on by
default for the project, so this line decides whether text appears in that beat.

  On-screen caption: Sunset Crossing Apartments      -> that text is burned in
  On-screen caption: None                            -> no caption on this beat

Write `None` (or leave the line out) wherever you do not want text — never leave it blank
after the colon while expecting a caption, and never put quotation marks around None.
If you want no burned-in captions anywhere, turn captions off for the whole project; the
SRT and the narration are unaffected by that setting.

WHAT THE STORYBOARD DOES WITH YOUR RANGES
It fits each beat's picture to its own narration clock and records every change:
shortens a range that is longer than the narration, extends one that is shorter inside
the same file, snaps OUT to a real frame. It never changes your script, never swaps in a
file you did not name, and never speeds up or repeats footage to cover a gap. If it
cannot fit a beat honestly, it stops and says which beat and why.

CHECK YOURSELF BEFORE YOU UPLOAD
The app will tell you all of this on upload, but fixing it here saves a round trip.

  1. Length of each file. Note each video's real duration. For every beat, Source out
     must be less than or equal to the length of the file named on its Full source line.
     This is the single mistake that stops a storyboard being built.
  2. Span arithmetic. For narration: Narration out − Narration in must equal
     Source out − Source in, and equal Master out − Master in. For a bite:
     Source out − Source in must equal the Duration you wrote.
  3. Narration clock. First beat starts at 0. Each bite's PAUSED time is the previous
     narration's OUT. Each RESUMES begins at the same PAUSED time. The final narration
     OUT equals your audio duration to within a quarter second.
  4. Master clock. Each beat's Master in is the previous beat's Master out, and the last
     Master out is your TOTAL MASTER RUNTIME.
  5. Filenames. Every Full source line is an exact uploaded filename, used the same way
     in SOURCES at the top.
  6. Table. Every block starts where the previous ended, block 1 starts at zero, and the
     last block ends at your audio duration.
  7. Captions. Every beat has an On-screen caption line: the literal text, or None.

If numbers 1 to 4 hold, the storyboard can be built and the only remaining work is
editorial review.

AFTER YOU UPLOAD
1. Upload the narration audio first, then the script (or the .srt), then every source.
2. Read the panel. Anything it lists here is a real conflict with your files.
3. Open "Review the Beat picture plan". Read the picture-range changes and the repeated
   footage list, then build.
4. Review the timeline, then check the pictures.

The app never edits your script, invents a source, or decides identity, chronology,
speaker or reuse rights. Those stay your review.
